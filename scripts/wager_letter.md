# The trickle-down wager comes due

On {frozen_on}, a page in this repo made a falsifiable claim and picked a date.
The date is today.

**The claim.** An entry-level model — the slot a vendor designates as its
cheapest — would match {baseline}, the frontier as of June 2026, on the
Artificial Analysis intelligence index at no more than a tenth of {baseline}'s
price: at or below ${price_cap} per million tokens on the fixed 3:1 blend.

**The two dates.** {method_b_date} from the price-decline lens, {method_a_date}
from the historical-lag lens. The earlier of the two is today.

Two illustrative scenarios, not a forecast. They landed close together because
capability diffusion and price decline are two views of the same process — that
was never two independent estimates agreeing, and neither date was good to
better than a season. The historical-lag lens rested on {n_pairs} pairs
produced by only {n_matchers} catch-up releases.

**Today is the forecast, not the deadline.** The wager resolves NO only if
nothing qualifies by 23:59 UTC on {deadline}. Passing today unmatched costs the
forecast, not the claim — so read the checklist below as a status check with
{deadline} still ahead, not as a verdict.

**Where it stood when the wager was frozen.** {baseline} scored
{baseline_aa} on the Artificial Analysis intelligence index. The best any
entry-level model managed was {closest} at {closest_aa} — {gap} points short.

secondary check and the index is the one binding arm.

**Read it.** The terms are in `wager.json`; nothing about them is negotiable
now, which was the point of writing them down first. Note that the arm is a
joint event — the index AND a tenth of the price, on the same model, at the
same time. Matching the index alone does not settle it.

Two ways this ends badly, both worth checking for before declaring a winner.
The wager could resolve YES on a technicality — an index version change that

wrong reason: the trend held perfectly well and the vendors stopped shipping a
